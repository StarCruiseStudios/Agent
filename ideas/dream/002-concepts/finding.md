# Concept: Finding

**Layer:** 002, Concepts
**Status:** Proposed

A finding is an [inference](inference.md) about the record, identified individually and linked to its [evidence](evidence.md). It is a more abstract connection than an [association](association.md), drawn from records and the associations between them. A finding can rest on many pieces of evidence, and one piece of evidence can be linked to many findings. A finding either holds up against its evidence or does not, and one that does not hold up stays in [internal knowledge](internal-knowledge.md) with the reasons.

## Kinds

Layer 001 gives three kinds of finding as examples, and the set of kinds is open.

- A **pattern** is a failure that keeps coming back, or an approach that keeps working, across sessions.
- A **contradiction** is two or more claims that cannot all hold in the same period.
- **Stale knowledge** is a claim, often one already published in an output, that held for an earlier period and no longer holds.

## Sameness

Two findings are the same when Archon judges that they state the same inference about the record. The judgment is itself an inference and can be revised.
